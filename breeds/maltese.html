Common Dental Issues

Coat Hides Early Signs

The thick white fur around a Maltese's face frequently obscures the mouth, making visual checks difficult and delaying detection of dental problems.

Tiny Jaw and Crowding

Like most toy breeds, Maltese have a very small jaw with tightly packed teeth that create zones impossible to clean properly at home.

Rapid Tartar Formation

Maltese form tartar quickly, especially on the back teeth which are the most difficult to reach during brushing.

Persistent Bad Breath

Persistent bad breath in a Maltese almost always indicates active bacterial accumulation — not normal dog breath.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my Maltese have bad breath?

Persistent bad breath in a Maltese almost always indicates active bacterial infection — not normal dog breath. A vet dental exam is recommended.

How do I check my Maltese's teeth through all that fur?

Gently part the facial fur at the mouth line and lift the lips. Check gum colour, yellowing and any brown deposits on the teeth.

How often should a Maltese have a professional dental cleaning?

Every 6 months from age 1. Maltese often develop periodontal disease before age 3.

What size dental chews are safe for a Maltese?

XS size only. Standard-size chews are a choking hazard for Maltese.
