Common Dental Issues

Severe Brachycephalic Jaw Compression

Pugs have severe brachycephalic jaw compression that forces teeth to rotate and overlap in ways that are structurally impossible to clean properly at home.

Limited Mouth Opening

Many Pugs cannot open their mouth wide enough for effective brushing. Home cleaning can only partially compensate.

Deep Facial Skin Folds

Deep skin folds near the mouth trap bacteria that contribute to oral infections and accelerate dental disease.

Rapid Disease Progression

Even with good home care, Pugs deteriorate quickly without regular professional cleaning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Pugs have dental problems if they're medium-sized?

The severe brachycephalic jaw structure forces teeth to rotate and overlap. Many Pugs can't open their mouth wide enough for effective brushing.

Can I brush my Pug's teeth with a normal toothbrush?

A regular brush is generally too rigid. A silicone finger brush is a better option, complemented by a no-brush dental gel for inaccessible areas.

How often does a Pug need a professional dental cleaning?

Every 6 months — this is more critical for Pugs than for almost any other breed due to structural limitations.

What dental products work for flat-face dogs like Pugs?

TropiClean gel applied directly to the gums without brushing, ProDen PlaqueOff powder in food, and bluestem water additive provide continuous protection.
