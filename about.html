Hi, I'm Puffy 🐩

A 7-year-old Toy Poodle who lost 9 teeth to preventable dental disease — 6 at age 5, and 3 more at age 7. My story is why DentiPaws exists.

How it happened

Years 1 – 4

Everything looked fine on the surface.

Annual checkups, occasional brushing, dental chews when we remembered. My teeth looked okay to the naked eye. What nobody saw was that disease was already forming below my gumline — in the 60% of the mouth that only X-rays under anaesthesia can reach.

Year 5 — First surgery

Six teeth extracted. A devastating day.

My breath had changed — off, fishy. My humans booked a proper dental exam with X-rays under anaesthesia. The news was bad: severe periodontal disease, and 6 teeth too far gone to save. Six extractions in one surgery. They drove home in silence.

Years 5 – 6

Everything changed — but the damage was already done.

After that surgery my humans brushed my teeth every day without exception. Water additive in the bowl, dental chews. They did everything right. But years of silent bone loss couldn't be reversed, even with perfect care going forward.

Year 7 — Second surgery

Three more extractions.

The bone loss from those early years had progressed beyond saving. Three more teeth had to come out. Nine total — all before my 8th birthday. Another anaesthetic, more stitches, more recovery.

Today

Happy and loved — with consequences.

I manage fine. Soft food is easier now and some toys aren't what they used to be, but I've adapted. My humans have moved past the guilt. They didn't know what they didn't know. That's exactly why this site exists.

What would have made the difference: As a Toy Poodle, my tiny jaw packed a full set of adult teeth into too small a space — constant plaque traps. Daily brushing from puppyhood, combined with professional dental exams including X-rays every six months, would have caught everything while it was still reversible. Nine teeth is what happens when years pass without a consistent routine.

Why DentiPaws exists

After my second surgery, my human spent weeks reading every veterinary study she could find. She learned that dental disease is one of the most common — and most preventable — conditions in dogs. She found that only a small fraction of owners brush consistently. That the fishy breath she'd noticed wasn't cosmetic — it was active bacterial infection.

The research existed. But nobody had made it genuinely accessible for regular dog owners. Nobody had simply said: "Your breed is high risk. Here is what to do. Start today."

So she built it. For me. For every dog who still has time.
