Dog Dental Insurance Compared — Is It Worth It?

🐩 By Puffy — DentiPaws 📅 July 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read

A routine professional dental cleaning under anaesthesia costs $300–1000. A cleaning that uncovers teeth needing extraction can easily reach $1,500–3,000. Yet dental coverage is one of the most misunderstood areas of pet insurance — with exclusions buried in the small print that catch owners off guard at exactly the wrong moment. Here's what you actually need to know before you buy.

Why dental coverage is complicated

Most pet insurance policies treat dental care very differently from other veterinary care. The key distinction almost every policy makes is between dental illness (disease, infection, periodontal disease) and dental accidents (fractured tooth from trauma). Basic policies typically cover only dental accidents. Comprehensive policies may cover dental illness — but almost always with conditions attached, such as requiring a dental exam within a set period of taking out the policy, or excluding pre-existing dental conditions. Periodic professional cleanings (preventive care) are excluded from the vast majority of insurance policies. Some providers offer wellness add-ons that reimburse a portion of preventive dental costs — these are worth examining separately.

The pre-existing condition problem

This is where most owners get caught out. If your dog has any evidence of dental disease at the time you take out a policy — even just minor tartar noted on an exam — that condition will typically be classified as pre-existing and excluded from coverage. For small breeds, which often have dental disease developing by age 3, this means many dogs are already partially or fully excluded by the time their owners think to insure them. The practical implication: If you have a puppy or young dog, especially a high-risk small breed, insure them before their first professional dental exam if possible. Once disease is documented in their records, your coverage options narrow significantly.

Comparing the major providers

Healthy Paws
Covers dental illness (not just accidents) under its standard plan. No annual or per-incident limits — coverage is based on actual costs after deductible and co-insurance. Does not offer a wellness add-on for preventive cleaning. Pre-existing conditions excluded. Premiums from ~20$-$83/month depending on annual payout limit, reimbursement %, annual deductible sum and breed.
Trupanion
Covers dental disease including periodontal disease treatment and extractions — one of the more generous dental policies. Usually coverage includes treatment for all new dental illnesses and injuries, but only if the dog has annual dental exam and the veterinarian’s recommended dental care is followed. Preventive dental and pre-existing conditions not covered. Premiums vary significantly by breed and age.
Nationwide
Offers a comprehensive plan that includes dental illness. They offer coverage for conditions relating to teeth and mouth illness (such as periodontitis and abscessed teeth), accidents/injuries (fractured teeth or trauma), or procedures used to treat these conditions (extractions or root canals) as long as the condition is not pre-existing to the policy effective date.
Embrace
Offers a $1000 dental coverage as a standard feature, but focuses on focuses on accidents and illnesses, including serious dental conditions, extraction. Unfortunately, routine wellness care like teeth cleanings are not covered. However some optional wellness plans may provide coverage for these services.
Petsecure
Offers built-in dental coverage across all its accident and illness especially in Canada, helping pay for extractions, oral surgery, and routine dental care up to $600 per year depending on the chosen plan.

What a real claim looks like

To make this concrete: Let's say a dog's dental surgery — four extractions, full anaesthesia, X-rays, post-operative care — totalled approximately $2,200. Under a typical comprehensive policy (a large proportion reimbursement, $250 annual deductible):

  • Total bill: $2,200
  • Minus deductible: $1,950 claimable
  • a large proportion reimbursement: $1,560 paid by insurance
  • Owner pays: $640 out of pocket
Without insurance: $2,200 out of pocket. For a dog with two major dental surgeries over a lifetime, comprehensive dental insurance typically pays for itself many times over — provided the policy was taken out before disease was documented.

The wellness add-on question

Wellness add-ons that partially reimburse preventive dental cleaning are worth calculating carefully. A typical wellness add-on costs $15–30/month (~$180–360/year) and reimburses $75–200 toward dental cleaning. If your dog needs annual professional cleaning at $400–600, the math can work in your favour — particularly for high-risk small breeds that genuinely need yearly cleanings. However: wellness add-ons rarely cover the full cost of cleaning, and they won't help with the expensive extractions and disease treatment that represent the real financial risk. Don't choose a policy primarily for its wellness dental benefit at the expense of comprehensive illness coverage.

💡 Key Takeaway

Insure early — before dental disease is documented. Look for policies that cover dental illness, not just accidents. For high-risk small breeds, comprehensive coverage with dental illness included is worth the higher premium. Calculate the wellness add-on against your actual preventive cleaning costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does pet insurance cover dog dental cleaning?

Most standard pet insurance policies do not cover routine professional dental cleaning. Some providers offer wellness add-ons that partially reimburse preventive cleaning costs ($75–200 per year), but these are purchased separately and rarely cover the full cost. Always read the fine print — 'dental coverage' in most policies refers to dental illness or accident treatment, not preventive cleaning under anaesthesia.

What pet insurance covers dental disease in dogs?

Providers that cover dental illness (not just accidents) include Trupanion, Healthy Paws, Nationwide Whole Pet plan, Embrace, and Figo Complete plan. Coverage terms, exclusions, and premiums vary significantly. The most important factor is whether the policy covers periodontal disease treatment and extractions — read each policy's dental exclusions carefully before purchasing.

Is dental insurance worth it for small breed dogs?

Often yes — particularly for high-risk small breeds (Chihuahua, Toy Poodle, Pug, French Bulldog). These breeds frequently require professional cleaning annually and may need costly extractions. A single dental surgery with extractions can cost $1,500–3,000. A comprehensive policy costing $40–60 per month that covers dental illness typically pays for itself after one significant dental procedure.

What is the pre-existing condition problem with dog dental insurance?

If your dog has any evidence of dental disease documented in veterinary records before you take out a policy, that condition is typically excluded as pre-existing. For small breeds that commonly develop dental disease by age 3, this means insuring early — ideally before the first professional dental examination — is essential to secure full coverage. Once disease is documented, coverage options narrow significantly.

How much does a dog dental cleaning cost without insurance?

A routine professional dental cleaning under anaesthesia typically costs $300–800, depending on location and clinic. If extractions are needed, costs rise to $1,500–3,000 or more depending on the number of teeth and complexity. Annual or twice-yearly cleanings recommended for high-risk breeds represent a significant ongoing cost — which is why dental insurance or a dedicated savings fund is worth planning for.
